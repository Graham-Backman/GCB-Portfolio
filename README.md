## ABOUT ME:
I’m a progressive minded researcher with experience in survey methodology, data analysis and communication for laymen audiences. For the past four years (2022-2026), I have served as a Research Fellow for the UMass Poll, the university’s survey research institute. In this capacity I write and edit survey questions and experiments for both statewide and national surveys. My research focuses on how political campaign's use racial messaging to influence voter belief and behavior.

## Working Projects
Is There A Race Card in the Deck? Implicit and Explicit Racial Appeals in Modern Presidential Campaigns  – Solo Authorship
      - **My Honors Thesis**: Analysis of all official presidential candidate campaign ads in the 2012, 2016, and 2020 races tracking the use of racial appeals. Contrary to the expectations of theory that racial appeals are used to bolster white turnout, I find a limited use of racial appeals with the most prevalent being not present in theory with campaign ads appealing to minority voters.
      - It is my intention to continue this project, perhaps in a PhD program, and extend our theories around racial political communication beyond Mendelberg (2001)
      
The Influence of anti-Latino Implicit and Explicit Racial Appeals in Candidate Evaluation - with Tatishe Nteta
      - Survey experiment with chosen city names acting as primes for race
      
Color-Blind to Change? How Whites and People of Color Talk about Rising Diversity – with Seth Goldman and Bel Corder
      - Open text responses from a panel survey with over samples of people of color after being told about America becoming a 'majority minority nation'. Responses vary from color-blind, color-concious, to exclusionary.
